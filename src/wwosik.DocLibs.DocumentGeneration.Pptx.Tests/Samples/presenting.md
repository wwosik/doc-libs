## Every stop on the route {transition="wipe"}

- Stop 1: the northern pier, loading containers for the coastal line
- Stop 2: the customs office, where papers are checked before departure
- Stop 3: the fuel dock, which opens at six and closes at ten
- Stop 4: the eastern basin, used for repairs and inspections only
- Stop 5: the ferry terminal, served every hour in the summer months
- Stop 6: the grain silos, loading bulk cargo for the inland routes
- Stop 7: the cold store, for fresh fish and fruit from the south
- Stop 8: the container yard, the largest area of the whole harbour
- Stop 9: the cruise quay, busy on weekends from May to September
- Stop 10: the pilot station, where every large ship takes a pilot on board
- Stop 11: the outer breakwater, with the lighthouse at its end
- Stop 12: the marina, for small boats and the harbour authority's launches
- Stop 13: the shipyard, building two ferries for the coastal line this year
- Stop 14: the old warehouse district, now offices and a museum
- Stop 15: the tug berth, where four tugs wait for the large ships
- Stop 16: the ro-ro ramp, loading trucks and trailers for the islands
- Stop 17: the bunker station, refuelling ships at anchor in the roads
- Stop 18: the timber quay, stacked with logs from the northern forests
- Stop 19: the harbour office, open every day of the year from six to six
- Stop 20: the rail yard, linking the quays with the national network
- Stop 21: the passenger hall, with waiting rooms and a small café
- Stop 22: the dry dock, booked out for the next eight months
- Stop 23: the scrap quay, shipping metal for recycling abroad
- Stop 24: the southern pier, the last stop before the open sea
